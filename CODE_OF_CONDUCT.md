# Code of conduct

Takes is a small project run by one person in spare time. Everyone who takes part (in issues, pull requests, discussions, reviews and any other project space) is expected to follow this code.

## What we expect

- Be kind and assume good intent. Most people here are giving their free time.
- Disagree with ideas, not people. Explain your reasoning and accept that the maintainer makes the final call.
- Respect people's time. Search before you post, stay on topic, and give the details someone needs to help you.
- Welcome newcomers, people who are learning, and people who write in English as a second language.
- Credit other people's work, and say so when something you post was written with an AI tool (see [CONTRIBUTING.md](CONTRIBUTING.md#ai-assisted-contributions)).

## What isn't acceptable

- Harassment, insults, slurs, or personal or political attacks.
- Sexual language or imagery, and unwelcome attention of any kind.
- Discrimination based on age, body, disability, ethnicity, gender identity or expression, experience, nationality, appearance, race, religion, sexual identity or orientation, or anything else about who someone is.
- Publishing someone's private information, including messages or drafts they shared to report a bug, without their permission.
- Spam: mass-generated issues or pull requests, repeated "+1" or "any update?" comments, and self-promotion.
- Continuing an interaction after someone has asked you to stop.

## Reporting

If someone breaks this code, report it privately. Don't argue it out in public.

- Use GitHub's **Report content** option in the menu (⋯) on the comment, issue or pull request. It reaches GitHub, and the maintainer is told that something was reported.
- Security problems aren't conduct reports. Follow [SECURITY.md](SECURITY.md) instead.

Reports are handled as privately as possible. Nobody who reports in good faith will be penalised for it.

## What happens next

The maintainer reviews every report and may, depending on how serious and how repeated the behaviour is:

1. Ask the person, privately, to change how they behave.
2. Hide or edit the content, or lock the conversation.
3. Block the person temporarily from the repository.
4. Block the person permanently.

This code applies in all project spaces, and also when someone represents the project elsewhere.
